Overview

While exchange rate fluctuations happen in global currency markets, their ripple effects hit domestic grocery shelves — systematically driving up national headline inflation and creating sticky food price hikes that refuse to come down even when the peso recovers.

As an import-dependent archipelago, the Philippines relies heavily on foreign-denominated fuel, fertilizers, wheat, feed inputs, and food commodities. Understanding how PHP/USD exchange rate shocks propagate across 18 administrative regions and 13 food subcategories is vital for timely monetary and agricultural policy intervention.

Problem

When the Philippine Peso depreciates against the US Dollar, households experience rising living costs. However, three empirical questions remain contested: (1) how strongly annual peso depreciation tracks national headline CPI, (2) whether inter-island shipping sensitivities cause unequal inflation spikes across Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao, and (3) how many months it takes for FX shocks to hit retail Food CPI.

Solution

Using overlapping PSA Consumer Price Index and BSP PHP/USD exchange rate series (2022–2026), we conduct normality-guided non-parametric and parametric hypothesis testing (Spearman ρ, Pearson r, Kruskal-Wallis H, One-Way ANOVA, and Lagged OLS Pass-Through Modeling) to isolate national, regional, and food-sector sensitivity.

Background

Imported Inflation, Archipelagic Supply Chains, and Downward Price Rigidity

Between 2022 and 2026, the Philippine economy navigated turbulent global monetary tightening, energy cost volatility, and recurring currency depreciation waves pushing the USD/PHP exchange rate from ₱51.30 to over ₱61.00. Research Questions & Statistical Hypotheses

RESEARCH QUESTION 01 Reject H₀

"What is the statistical relationship between the rate of peso depreciation and the overall growth of the Philippine Consumer Price Index (CPI)?"

Null Hypothesis (H₀)

Peso depreciation does not correspond to a systematic change in the growth rate of the Philippine Consumer Index (CPI).

Alternative Hypothesis (H₁)

Peso depreciation corresponds to a systematic change in the growth rate of the Philippine Consumer Index (CPI).

Spearman ρ = 0.5718 p = 9.42 × 10⁻⁵
RESEARCH QUESTION 02 Fail to Reject H₀

"Is there a significant difference in how peso depreciation impacts the CPI across different regions in the Philippines?"

Null Hypothesis (H₀ — Supported)

There is no significant difference in the magnitude of CPI increase across different regions in the Philippines during periods of peso depreciation.

Alternative Hypothesis (H₁)

Some regions experience a significantly higher increase in CPI compared to others during periods of peso depreciation due to higher inter-island logistics sensitivity.

Kruskal-Wallis H = 0.5999 p = 0.7408 (> 0.05)
RESEARCH QUESTION 03 Reject H₀ (Lag 3–4m)

"How does the peso depreciation affect the Food CPI as a whole?"

Null Hypothesis (H₀: β₁ = 0)

Food CPI responds at a similar rate following peso depreciation (no significant pass-through elasticity or lagged differentiation).

Alternative Hypothesis (H₁: β₁ > 0)

Food CPI responds at different rates following peso depreciation, exhibiting strong index pass-through and significant 2–3 month delayed transmission.

OLS r = 0.8264 (p < 0.0001) Lag 3m r = 0.3487

01

Data Filtering & Variable Calculation

We computed Year-over-Year (YoY) growth rates for national aggregate CPI and exchange rate at month t relative to t−12, filtering out the initial 12-month baseline window (Jan–Dec 2022) and unreleased BSP exchange-rate observations (Jun–Aug 2026) to yield N = 41 overlapping months (Jan 2023 – May 2026):

CPIYoY = [ (CPIt − CPIt−12) / CPIt−12 ] × 100
FXYoY = [ (FXt − FXt−12) / FXt−12 ] × 100

For regional and food short-term dynamics, we calculated Month-over-Month (MoM) percentage growth rates and applied a Depreciation Months Filter (ΔFXMoM > 0) across 30 depreciation months and 18 regions (N = 540 total: Luzon = 240, Visayas = 120, Mindanao = 180):

CPIGrowthMoM (i,t) = [ (CPIi,t − CPIi,t−1) / CPIi,t−1 ] × 100

Kruskal-Wallis H-Test & Dynamic Lagged OLS Pass-Through

Regional equality across island groups (k = 3, N = 540) and all 18 regions (df = 17) was tested using the Kruskal-Wallis rank-sum statistic, while Food CPI elasticity and transmission delays (Lag 0m to Lag 6m) were modeled via Ordinary Least Squares (OLS):

H = [ 12 / (N(N + 1)) ] × ∑ (Rj² / nj) − 3(N + 1)

Conclusion & Policy Synthesis

Exchange Rate Shocks Are a Systemic National Burden With 3-Month Lagged, Sticky Food Inflation

By uniting parametric and non-parametric hypothesis testing across national, regional, and food-sector CPI datasets from 2022 to 2026, our study delivers three actionable empirical conclusions for Philippine macroeconomic policy:

01

Direct Macroeconomic Pass-Through

Annual peso depreciation significantly drives up national headline CPI (ρ = 0.5718, r = 0.6037, p < 0.001). Stabilizing foreign exchange volatility is inseparable from domestic price stability.

02

Nationwide Regional Uniformity

During currency depreciation months, CPI growth across Luzon (0.432%), Visayas (0.425%), and Mindanao (0.437%) is statistically indistinguishable (H = 0.5999, p = 0.7408). Anti-inflation relief must be nationwide rather than regionally siloed.

03

The 90-Day Intervention Window

Because Food CPI pass-through peaks at a 2 to 3 month lag (r = 0.3487, p = 0.0141) and exhibits downward price stickiness, policymakers have a critical 60-to-90-day window after an FX shock to deploy targeted agricultural and food import buffers before retail prices lock in.
