Unequal Prices?

Analyzing the impact of currency depreciation on regional inflation in the Philippines

CS 132 · Group 5 · ICPING EKONOMIYA · SDG 8: Decent Work and Economic Growth

56months, Jan 2022 – Aug 2026
18administrative regions
14CPI items (All Items + 13 food categories)
14,112panel observations

Background and motivation

The peso is not stable against the US dollar. In our dataset, the monthly average rate moved from about ₱51.2 per USD in January 2022 to about ₱61.4 per USD in May 2026. A weaker peso makes imported goods and imported inputs, such as fuel, fertilizer, and feed, more expensive, and that cost can pass through to consumer prices.

That pass-through may not be even. The Philippines is an archipelago, and goods reach different regions through different inter-island supply chains. Two regions facing the same exchange-rate shock could therefore see different price increases. National inflation figures hide this difference.

This project investigates how national exchange-rate volatility translates into unequal price burdens across the 18 administrative regions. It connects to SDG 8 because currency shocks and inflation affect macroeconomic stability and how evenly economic development reaches different parts of the country.

What we propose

We build a regional panel dataset that joins monthly PHP/USD exchange rates from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) with monthly regional Consumer Price Index (CPI) data from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA). We then compare how CPI responds to peso depreciation overall, across regions, and within food categories.

Research questions

RQ1What is the statistical relationship between the rate of peso depreciation and the overall growth of the Philippine Consumer Price Index (CPI)?
RQ2Is there a significant difference in how peso depreciation impacts the CPI across different regions in the Philippines?
RQ3How does peso depreciation affect the Food CPI as a whole?

Research hypotheses

RQ1

H₀: Peso depreciation does not correspond to a systematic change in the growth rate of the Philippine Consumer Price Index (CPI).

H₁: Peso depreciation corresponds to a systematic change in the growth rate of the Philippine Consumer Price Index (CPI).

RQ2

H₀: There is no significant difference in the magnitude of CPI increase across different regions in the Philippines during periods of peso depreciation.

H₁: Some regions experience a significantly higher increase in CPI compared to others during periods of peso depreciation, due to higher inter-island logistics sensitivity.

RQ3

H₀: Food CPI responds at a similar rate following peso depreciation.

H₁: Food CPI responds at different rates following peso depreciation.

Objectives

  1. Construct a dataset of PHP/USD exchange rates and monthly regional CPI data from 2022 to 2026 across all 18 administrative regions.
  2. Compare the magnitude of CPI growth across regions during periods of peso depreciation.
  3. Evaluate price response rates and trends within the Food CPI category following peso depreciation.

Collection process and sampling

We did not collect prices ourselves. The underlying figures come from the agencies' own collection: the PSA's retail price surveys and the BSP's tracking of interbank foreign exchange, which produces daily reference rates. We extracted the published data as two raw Excel spreadsheets, one per source, and integrated them into one dataset. No sampling was applied. We use all available monthly observations for all 18 regions and all 14 items from January 2022 to August 2026.

Preprocessing

  1. Structural cleaning and unpivoting. The raw spreadsheets were reshaped into long format with one row per region, item, and month (18 regions × 14 items).
  2. Key and string normalization. Temporal keys (year and month) and text labels were normalized across the 56 monthly periods so the two sources could be joined.
  3. Relational integration. The monthly average USD/PHP rate was attached to every region–item–month row, producing 14,112 panel records.
  4. Calculated fields. Food inflation (%) and peso depreciation (%) are derived from the CPI and exchange-rate columns.
